# BlogForge Narrated Whole-App Film Design

**Date:** July 30, 2026  
**Status:** Approved for production  
**Workflow:** `brag` with Hyperframes  
**Output directory:** `brag-output-2026-07-30-122331/`

## Objective

Create a 40–45 second narrated BlogForge product film that explains the app's primary purpose: helping a writer develop substantive long-form content from whatever they actually have, carry it through a coherent writing process, preserve and strengthen their voice, review AI-assisted prose with human judgment, improve discoverability for search and answer engines, and deliberately finish or publish the result.

This replaces feature-list storytelling with one chronological article journey.

## Audience and Central Message

The audience is founders, practitioners, consultants, technical writers, and other people publishing thoughtful work under their own names.

The central message is:

> BlogForge develops raw material into publishable long-form writing without taking the writer's voice or judgment out of the process.

The viewer should understand that BlogForge:

1. can begin with a topic, outline, notes, source material, an existing draft, or a blank page;
2. helps discover the angle and build a purposeful outline rather than mechanically mirroring the input;
3. composes and revises one coherent article;
4. uses a Voice Profile and inline tools to shape passages toward the writer's style;
5. treats Humanization Check as transparent editorial guidance, not AI-authorship detection;
6. provides Proofreader, Fact-check, Shape, and GEO review while leaving every change with the writer;
7. supports hero imagery, repurposing, export, and deliberate publication.

## Format and Tone

- Landscape, 1920 × 1080
- Target runtime: 44 seconds
- 30 frames per second
- Polished editorial product film
- Private, on-device Kokoro narration
- Existing restrained music bed and sparse interface sound effects
- Captions are not required; short on-screen phrases reinforce the narration
- The first and final frames must remain useful as thumbnail/poster candidates

## Locked Narration

> Good writing rarely starts with a perfect prompt. It starts with a topic, a rough outline, scattered notes, or source material. BlogForge helps develop that raw material into a real angle, a purposeful outline, and one coherent draft. Then it helps you write without losing what makes the work yours. Your Voice Profile guides the language, while inline tools reshape passages in your style. If AI helped, Humanization Check surfaces patterns worth revisiting—while you decide what changes. Proofreader, Fact-check, Shape, and GEO strengthen clarity, credibility, structure, and discoverability, including how answer engines understand the article. Finish with a hero, repurpose it, export, or publish deliberately. BlogForge: from something to say, to something worth publishing—in your voice.

The synthesized read determines the exact scene boundaries. If it exceeds 45 seconds, increase narration speed only within a natural range before cutting meaning.

## Storyboard

### Scene 1 — Writing begins before the prompt

Open on a blank generic prompt box. A topic card, rough outline, notes, and source cards gather around it as the line “Good writing starts with something to say” replaces the empty prompt.

**Narrative job:** Reframe the problem in the writer's language and establish that BlogForge begins with substance, not generation.

### Scene 2 — Start with what you have

Reconstruct BlogForge's real starting-point picker. Emphasize **Help me shape it**, **I have an outline**, and **I have source material**, while keeping the other supported modes visible.

**Narrative job:** Show that topic, outline, notes/sources, existing prose, and blank-page writing are all legitimate entries.

### Scene 3 — Develop the argument

Move through **Talk it through** into an outline workspace. Questions reveal audience, claim, tension, evidence, and desired conclusion. Source and note chips become a fresh outline whose sections follow the argument rather than the input document's headings.

**Narrative job:** Make content development—not generation—the centerpiece.

### Scene 4 — Compose one coherent draft

The approved outline folds into a single manuscript. The article's opening, middle, and conclusion connect with a visible argument thread and the real action **Compose full draft**.

**Narrative job:** Show that BlogForge creates and supports one article, not disconnected section fragments.

### Scene 5 — Make it sound like the writer

Reconstruct the Voice Profile and an inline **In my voice** edit. A generic passage becomes more direct, concrete, and rhythmically consistent with the profile.

**Narrative job:** Show voice as an active writing input and revision tool.

### Scene 6 — Humanize and strengthen

In the editor, Humanization Check opens one explainable pattern and a suggested revision. The writer explicitly applies it. The review rail then moves through **Proofreader**, **Fact-check**, **Shape**, and **GEO**. GEO visibly checks answer clarity, entity/context cues, and retrievability rather than implying keyword stuffing.

**Narrative job:** Explain the human-in-the-loop review model and give GEO meaningful screen time.

### Scene 7 — Finish and distribute deliberately

The article receives a hero image, then **Atomize all**, Markdown/HTML/Word export, and **Publish to GitHub** appear as writer-initiated next steps.

**Narrative job:** Complete the journey without depicting autonomous publication.

### Scene 8 — Brand close

The input cards, outline, manuscript, voice profile, review findings, and finished article resolve into one BlogForge workshop lockup:

> From something to say  
> to something worth publishing.  
> In your voice.

**Narrative job:** Restate the complete product promise, not a feature.

## Product-Fidelity Rules

- Humanization Check must never be described as AI-authorship detection.
- Fact-check must never be presented as a guarantee of factual accuracy.
- GEO may improve structure and retrievability for search and answer engines; it must not promise rankings or citations.
- Publishing is initiated and confirmed by the writer.
- Do not invent adoption, performance, or time-savings claims.
- Use actual BlogForge labels wherever interface copy is visible.

## Audio Direction

- Narrator: local Kokoro voice suited to a polished product demo, tested at a natural pace.
- Music: retain the existing clean editorial bed, reduced beneath narration.
- Duck music further under dense narration and allow slightly more presence in the opening and final hold.
- SFX: no more than five restrained cues, tied to meaningful UI actions.
- If the private narrator sounds materially distracting after a real draft render, deliver the same edit with music only rather than lower the film's quality.

## Acceptance Criteria

1. Runtime is between 40 and 45 seconds.
2. The narration clearly covers input options, content development, coherent drafting, voice shaping, humanization, editorial checks, GEO, finishing, and deliberate publication.
3. Content development and writing occupy more runtime than finish/distribution.
4. At least five scenes reconstruct recognizable BlogForge interface states.
5. All copy and claims follow the product-fidelity rules.
6. Narration is intelligible over the full music bed.
7. Hyperframes check passes with zero errors.
8. Key snapshots show readable settled states with no clipping or broken transitions.
9. The final MP4 is 1920 × 1080 with stereo audio and a 40–45 second duration.
10. A selected poster is exported as `brag.jpg` and baked into frame zero.
